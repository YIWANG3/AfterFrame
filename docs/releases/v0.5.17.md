AfterFrame 0.5.17 is a fix release. Dragging a large selection out of the gallery follows the pointer again, a file drop can no longer leave a window open for every file, and float and 32-bit TIFFs get a real preview. Underneath, the LUT tool, AI Repaint and Stickers each became a self-contained module; nothing about them changes in use.

## Download

| | File | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Mac, Apple Silicon** | `AfterFrame-0.5.17-arm64.dmg` | macOS 14 or later |
| **Mac, Apple Silicon, older macOS** | `AfterFrame-0.5.17-macOS12-arm64.dmg` | macOS 12 or 13 |
| **Mac, Intel** | `AfterFrame-0.5.17-macOS12-Intel.dmg` | macOS 12 or later |
| **Windows** | `AfterFrame-0.5.17-x64-setup.exe` | Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit |

The two macOS 12 builds are the same app without People, Scene Depth and Stickers, which use Apple's on-device models that need macOS 14. On an Apple Silicon Mac with macOS 14 or later, take the first one. The Intel build reads RAW files with an older LibRaw (0.21), which can't decode DNGs compressed with JPEG XL.

The Windows installer isn't signed yet. When Windows says "Windows protected your PC" (Windows 已保护你的电脑), click **More info** (更多信息), then **Run anyway** (仍要运行). To update, run the new installer: your catalogs and settings stay. LUTs, Stickers, People and Scene Depth are in the macOS app only for now.

A catalog opened by 0.5.13 or later can't be opened by 0.5.12 or older (the look-alike index added in 0.5.13). 0.5.17 doesn't change the catalog format.

## Fixes

### Dragging many photos keeps up with the pointer

On the Mac, dragging 50 or more photos out of the gallery left the pile of thumbnails and the drop highlight trailing the pointer, by hundreds of pixels at 150 photos. While the drag was over the window, every mouse move re-read every dragged file. The app now starts the drag itself and hands over the list of files once, so a drag of 200 photos moves like a drag of one.

**The drag icon is no longer washed out.** It used to be faded so that a hundred stacked copies stayed readable, which showed as banding on a large selection. Only one copy is drawn now.

### A dropped file no longer opens a window of its own

Adding photos by dropping them on the window could leave dozens of extra AfterFrame windows, each showing one photo. When a drop got past the page, the browser engine's default took over and opened every file in a new window; a single file would have replaced the app's own page. The app now refuses to open extra windows or to navigate away from itself, whatever a drop does.

### TIFFs that rendered black now have a preview

- **Float and 32-bit greyscale TIFFs** (an HDR merge, a depth map) came out as pure black and white. Their preview now stretches the image's own range.
- **Floating-point RGB and CMYK-with-alpha TIFFs** on the Mac rendered black or not at all. They now render.
- **The lightbox filmstrip shows a placeholder** for a photo nothing can render, instead of a broken-image icon.

### Under the hood

- **The LUT tool, AI Repaint and Stickers are feature modules.** Each keeps its panels, settings, main-process code, MCP tool and strings in one folder under `apps/desktop/features/`, described by a manifest that is checked when the app starts. This is groundwork for adding tools without touching the editor's core; `docs/feature-modules-plan.md` has the plan.
