AfterFrame 0.5.20 is about the shoot as a unit and about seeing more without leaving the photo: a folder dragged in from Finder becomes a folder in the library, deleting a folder can take its photos with it, the lightbox shows a photo's details beside it, and adding a tag in the inspector shows every tag you have.

## Download

| | File | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Mac, Apple Silicon** | `AfterFrame-0.5.20-arm64.dmg` | macOS 14 or later |
| **Mac, Apple Silicon, older macOS** | `AfterFrame-0.5.20-macOS12-arm64.dmg` | macOS 12 or 13 |
| **Mac, Intel** | `AfterFrame-0.5.20-macOS12-Intel.dmg` | macOS 12 or later |
| **Windows** | `AfterFrame-0.5.20-x64-setup.exe` | Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit |

The two macOS 12 builds are the same app without People, Scene Depth and Stickers, which use Apple's on-device models that need macOS 14. On an Apple Silicon Mac with macOS 14 or later, take the first one. The Intel build reads RAW files with an older LibRaw (0.21), which can't decode DNGs compressed with JPEG XL.

The Windows installer isn't signed yet. When Windows says "Windows protected your PC" (Windows 已保护你的电脑), click **More info** (更多信息), then **Run anyway** (仍要运行). To update, run the new installer: your catalogs and settings stay. LUTs, Stickers, People and Scene Depth are in the macOS app only for now.

A catalog opened by 0.5.19 or later can't be opened by 0.5.18 or older (the folder tree and tag groups added in 0.5.19). 0.5.20 doesn't change the catalog format.

## New

### One shoot, one folder

A shoot is already a folder on disk, and it used to take two steps to make it one in the library: import, then file. And a folder deleted after a shoot was scrapped or delivered left its photos behind in All Assets.

- **Drag a folder onto the sidebar's Folders** and its photos are imported and a folder of the same name is made for them. Dropped on a folder row, it becomes a subfolder of that folder. Loose files dropped on a folder row are imported and added to that folder.
- **Dropping the same folder again** makes neither a second folder nor second copies. Subfolders on disk are flattened into the one folder.
- When one folder was made, it opens, and the photos appear as the import goes.
- **Deleting a folder asks what becomes of its photos** when it holds photos that are in no other folder: **Delete Folder Only**, **Also Remove N Photos from Library** (the files stay on disk), or **Also Move N Files to Trash**. Photos that are in another folder too always stay, and the dialog says how many. Subfolders go with the folder and are counted. An empty folder still goes without a question.

Photos still live in the library and a folder is still a membership; these are two shortcuts, not a new model.

### Details beside the photo

Seeing what a photo was shot with meant leaving the lightbox for the grid. Now **I**, or the **Info** button in the lightbox's header, brings the photo's details out on the right.

- **The photo makes room.** The pane doesn't cover it: the photo, the header, the zoom bar, the filmstrip and the next arrow all move over, and the photo fits again. The pane sits where the grid's inspector sits, at the same width.
- **What it shows**: file name, rating, flag, size and type, camera and lens, exposure, dates, location, description and tags. Rating, flag, description and tags are edited right there. What the lightbox shows already, or what would lead to another photo, is left out.
- **A tag there** closes the lightbox and filters the grid by that tag; the location goes to the map.
- It is off until you open it, the choice is remembered, and it stays dark in the light theme, as the lightbox does. Proof hides it.
- The key is rebindable in **Settings → Keyboard Shortcuts** (**Show / Hide Info**).

### Every tag, when adding one

The inspector's **Add** used to offer a few suggestions. It now opens a picker with every tag in the library, under its group.

- **Click a tag to put it on the photo, click again to take it off.** The picker stays open for the next one.
- **Typing narrows the list**, by a tag's name, its name in the other language, or an alias. Enter adds what you typed; the group menu beside the box says where a new tag goes.
- A photo's tags always show their group's name, even when they are all in one group.

## Changed

- **The RAW filter is called Paired RAW.** Next to Format it read as "the file is a RAW", but it is about photos that have a RAW paired to them through Add RAW Source. Its options are **Has paired RAW** and **No paired RAW**, and the pinned entry in Discover and the collage's source filter use the same name. To see RAW files, use **Format**.
